## "What they built" (form field `idea`, 20-2000 characters)

That Invoice turns an invoice into a NEAR account. A business billing crypto clients waits 30-60 days to be paid; selling the invoice early means factoring, where the client still pays the original business, which must forward the money, and lenders worry the same invoice was sold twice. Here the payment address is the asset. The registry contract creates a real NEAR account per invoice (all running one immutable global contract) and mints the invoice as an NFT. The client confirms it on-chain; in the demo the client is a real Sputnik DAO that confirms and pays by proposal. The owner lists it at a discount, and a buyer's ft_transfer_call moves the NFT and pays the seller in one receipt. The client keeps paying the same address, and whoever owns the NFT then collects, so nothing is forwarded and the old owner cannot redirect the money. The contract refuses a repeated (issuer, debtor, number), locks the token during a sale or payout, handles money that arrives after collection, and keeps each client's payment record on-chain. Each NFT carries an SVG generated by the contract whose stamp follows its state. A visitor can create a real NEAR account in the browser, buy an invoice with their own key, and collect it. The registry's access keys are deleted; a script verifies that from chain data. Rust contracts and API (60 tests, CI green), Next.js UI, hosted on Fly. Everything runs on testnet with demo USDC; cross-chain payment via NEAR Intents is shown as live dry-run quotes.
